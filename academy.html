Set your target before you search

Clear targeting prevents a long list of businesses that do not match what you sell. Pick one service, one type of business, and one location for your first search.

Decide what you offer

  • Name one outcome: a website, SEO improvement, booking system, automation, or marketing support.
  • Choose the business types most likely to value that outcome.
  • Start with an honest service package you can deliver well.

Use a simple target brief

“I help business type in location improve outcome with service.”

Find businesses with meaningful signals

Use the Lead Engine to discover businesses by category and location, then use filters as prioritisation tools—not as a promise that every business matches perfectly.

Recommended first search

  1. Enter a business category and a city or region.
  2. Choose a realistic result count for your plan.
  3. Run the search, then review the returned data before saving.

How to read filters

  • Contact details only: favours records with available public contact data.
  • Missing website only: means no website was found in available provider data; verify before claiming there is no website.
  • Needs website: prioritises likely opportunity, not a confirmed business problem.

Qualify each opportunity before outreach

Prioritise relevance and evidence. A high score is a helpful signal; your own review decides whether a lead deserves your time.

Review in two minutes

  • Check business category, address, website and publicly listed phone number.
  • Open the website and note only what you can confirm.
  • Read score contributors and data completeness, not just the score.

Save useful context

  • Add a pipeline stage and a short note explaining why the business fits.
  • Record your proposed service and next action.
  • Do not duplicate a business that is already in your CRM.

Turn observations into a helpful website audit

Use a website audit to understand what is visible and actionable. Never describe a website as broken, insecure, or slow unless the audit gives you clear supporting evidence.

Useful observations

  • Does the site load and use HTTPS?
  • Is there a clear service explanation, call to action, and contact path?
  • Are mobile, metadata, or page-structure issues documented by the audit?

Make it client-ready

Write down one or two outcomes you can improve, such as clearer enquiries, better mobile navigation, or a more useful booking route. Link each recommendation to an observed finding.

Write outreach that earns a reply

AI can help you draft, but useful outreach is short, specific, respectful, and completely editable before you send anything.

A strong first message

  1. Introduce yourself and the service outcome in one sentence.
  2. Mention one confirmed, relevant observation.
  3. Offer one clear next step: a short call, a free review, or a tailored idea.

Keep it professional

  • Do not claim a relationship that does not exist.
  • Do not invent website problems or personal details.
  • Respect local messaging, privacy, and anti-spam laws. Stop if a contact asks you to.

Package your work and deliver confidently

Once there is genuine interest, show a clear scope, price, timeline, and next step. Build only what you can maintain and support.

Proposal essentials

  • Business goal and the agreed project scope.
  • Deliverables, timeline, revisions, and what is not included.
  • Price, currency, payment milestones, and a simple approval action.

Website Studio workflow

Create a professional first draft, review every statement and contact detail, then customise the design before sharing. A generated site is a draft—not a substitute for checking the final result.

Follow up, learn, and make it repeatable

A sustainable pipeline comes from reliable follow-through, useful records, and reviewing what actually happened—not from chasing vanity numbers.

Weekly rhythm

  • Review scheduled follow-ups and overdue work each morning.
  • Move every saved lead to its real pipeline stage.
  • Record only confirmed replies, meetings, proposals, and won revenue.

Improve the system

  • Review industries, locations, and offers that produce real meetings.
  • Reuse good search setups with Saved Searches.
  • Use the Daily Action Centre to decide what to do next.
